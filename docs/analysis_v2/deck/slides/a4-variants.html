Three separate things change with a variant. (1) The number of A/Ts. (2) What the caller uses: plasmid-LLR keeps only reference A/T positions where the read matches the reference, so any substitution or deletion at an A/T drops that base, and gained or inserted adenines are never used. A dropped base carries a median 11% (at most 25%) of the evidence within ±10 bp. (3) Neighbour visibility: breaking AAAAA into AAGAA makes the remaining adenines much easier to mark, so 63A>G is predicted to add 2.4 visible adenines while removing one A/T. The prediction uses naked-DNA rates from another plasmid; A7 measures these effects on our own WT molecules, and B1 (naked controls of this library) replaces the prediction.
