How to read the variant view

Accessible / inaccessible
An adenine with an m6A mark was reached by Hia5: accessible. One without a mark is inaccessible, either covered by a protein or simply missed (Hia5 marks some sequences poorly, e.g. inside A runs).
Footprint call
A stretch of a single molecule called protected by the caller. Confident: its inaccessible adenines would all be inaccessible by chance less than 0.1% of the time at that molecule's labeling. Faded calls could easily be chance.
WT footprints (F01-F24)
Recurring calls in WT molecules with the same midpoint and length: peaks of the WT call map. Boxes show their typical span; darker = more of their calls are confident.
Molecules
40 molecules each for WT and the variant (labeling ≥ 0.2, least labeled at the bottom). Bars are footprint calls: inside a WT footprint, other 10-80 bp call, 81+ bp (nucleosome-sized). Dots are accessible adenines.
Footprint occupancy
Share of molecules with a call in each WT footprint: WT, variant. WT is weighted to the variant's labeling mix.
Footprint change
WT − variant occupancy for each footprint, percentage points, with a ±1.96 SE bar. significant (false-discovery rate 5% across that variant's footprints); grey otherwise. Positive = the variant has the footprint less often.
Inaccessible fraction
Per adenine, share of molecules where it is inaccessible: WT, variant.
Inaccessibility change
WT − variant per adenine, ±1.96 SE. significant (A10 test); × = masked, not tested. Positive = more accessible in the variant.
Mask
Adenines hidden from both WT and variant before comparing, so the variant's own sequence change cannot fake a difference. A7: the changed base and neighbours whose A-run context changes. ±5 bp: every adenine within 5 bp (strict; short footprints near the variant become untestable).
Grey band
±10 bp around the variant: changes here may still be Hia5 sequence preference until naked-DNA controls exist.
