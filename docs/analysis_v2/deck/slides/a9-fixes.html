Step A9 · Two checks failed, two fixes (post hoc)

The planned clone test was overconfident; rare events broke the normal approximation

1 · Clone "sandwich" test → random effects

Seen: on HEK293T pseudo-variants, 57% of null tests had p < 0.05 (λ 11). Its standard error was ~1/6 of the plain binomial one.

Why: it learns clone-to-clone spread from the SNV's own clones. When one or two clones carry most molecules, there is nothing to learn from and the error is far too small (a known failure with few, unequal clusters; Cameron & Miller 2015).

Fix: learn the clone spread (τ²) from WT clones instead. A8's clone bootstrap has the same weakness, so it is also replaced for variant tests.

2 · Rare events → test only when ≥ 5 are expected

Seen: in HepG2 the random-effects test was right on average (4.9% at p < 0.05) but 8.3% of pseudo-variants had a BH discovery. Most sat at oligo 61–62, inside the A run 59–65, where WT is unmarked ~100% of the time (z down to −20).

Why: with a rate near 0 or 1, one or two events look enormous to a normal approximation.

Fix: test a feature only when the SNV's molecules are expected to show ≥ 5 of the rarer outcome (the usual rule for this approximation).

Both changes were made after seeing WT-only calibration and before any variant chromatin endpoint was computed · rules.json statistics.amendment_1 and amendment_2

The prespecified fallback (empirical p-values ranked against pseudo-variants) was not used for HepG2: with 4 draws per SNV its resolution (~1/2,500 per tier) cannot reach the BH thresholds for 101–844 features. It is used for HEK293T's motif-level tests in A10, with ≥ 50 draws per SNV.
